**Orcs** are a humanoid people native to the cold coasts and highlands of Arcanthys, built for endurance and combat and shaped, in their own tradition, by the god said to have forged them from the earth itself. Orc society is a martial meritocracy: rank, respect, and leadership are earned through strength and deed rather than inherited, a structure drawn directly from the doctrine of [[Gorvath Iron-Blood, the First Victor|Gorvath Iron-Blood]]. Their history is defined above all by the [[Unbroken Host]], the civilization that rose after the [[Age of the Deep]] receded and later tore itself apart from within.

## Physiology

Orcs are powerfully built, standing taller and heavier than most humans, with ash-grey to deep umber skin often compared to cooled metal or scorched stone. Lower tusks, heavy brows, and coarse dark hair are near universal. Hair and beards are commonly worn long and braided, knotted through with iron rings, teeth, or bone chips taken from past trials, so that an orc's braids can often be read like a rough tally of what they have survived. Scarring is treated the same way: not hidden, not regretted, and read as proof of trials rather than misfortune. Orcs who reach old age unscarred are viewed with quiet suspicion rather than admiration.

## Culture

### Faith of the Forge

Most orcs, devout or lapsed, are raised on the doctrine of Gorvath Iron-Blood: that no one is born complete, that every orc carries an "Inner Ore" refined only through hardship, and that stagnation, not defeat, is the true failure. Priests known as the Ironbound preside over duels, judge whether a death was worthy, and reforge the weapons of the fallen. Interpretation of the doctrine varies sharply between sects, from the aggressive expansionism of the Pure Iron, to the disciplined restraint of the Tempered Path, to the radical culling advocated by the Ashborn, and these disagreements have driven much of orc civil conflict, including the Final Proving itself.

Orcs who die well are believed to pass into the [[Hall of the Worthy|Hall of the Worthy]], Gorvath's afterlife, where combat continues as celebration rather than trial. Orcs speak of this fate plainly and without dread; a warrior's funeral is closer to a boast than a mourning.

### Holds and Clans

Without a central nation, orc society is organized by clan and hold rather than realm. A hold is built around a great meadhall, the seat of a chief and the gathering place for feasts, trials, and disputes. Leadership is never assumed to be permanent; any chief can be challenged for their seat, and a challenge is settled openly before the hold rather than resolved in private. Clans without land of their own often live and travel as raiding companies, following whichever chief currently commands the most respect.

Orc raiding companies travel by land, striking fast and withdrawing overland well ahead of pursuit. Crossing water is treated as a last resort rather than a route; a raiding party will go far out of its way to ford a river or take a bridge rather than risk a boat, and a hold reachable only by water is, in practice, far safer from orc raids than one that can be reached on foot.

### Fear of the Deep

Orcs carry a cultural unease around deep, open water that predates any individual memory of why. It is widely traced to the [[Age of the Deep]], when the ancestors of the orcs lived confined to the world's highest ground while krakens and the Drowning Deep ruled everything below. Even now, orcs avoid boats of any kind wherever they can help it, preferring to ford a river, take a bridge, or walk far out of their way rather than trust their weight to open water. Those who take to boats anyway are often regarded as having something to prove, or something wrong with them.

## Naming Customs

An orc's birth name marks them as a child of their clan, typically paired with a lineage mark such as "child of [parent's name]," and this name is kept for life. Unlike some peoples, orcs do not treat their given name as something to be outgrown or replaced. What can change is how that name is spoken of by others.

Great deeds are not declared by the orc who performed them. They are composed, exaggerated, and carried by skalds: wandering or hold-bound poets who preserve genealogy, feud, and glory alike in verse. A skald's song may attach a byname to a warrior, such as "Grosh Bonebraid" or "Umbaz who broke the river-gate," and that byname spreads only as far as the song does. An orc cannot claim their own byname with any credibility. It has to be sung by someone else first, and it can just as easily be a mockery that sticks as an honor that does.

## Relations with Other Peoples

Orc attitudes toward other peoples are colored heavily by Gorvath's doctrine. Elves are regarded as stagnant, their long lives and refinement read as a failure to ever truly test themselves. Dwarves earn genuine respect for their toughness and craft, tempered by pity that they prize the forge over the fight. Humans are considered unpredictable and full of potential, but are often seen as squandering it. These are cultural generalizations rather than fixed law, and individual orcs vary as widely as any other people, but they shape first impressions on both sides.

## History

### The Unbroken Host

Following the binding of [[Uru’gol, The Open Maw]] and the retreat of the seas at the close of the [[Age of the Deep]], orcs, until then nomadic, settled the newly exposed coasts and highlands in growing numbers. Holds became towns, and towns became the [[Unbroken Host]], a civilization built on open competition, where the strongest and most capable rose and standing was never assumed to be permanent.

The Host's decline came from within. [[Kharzug One-Eye]], preaching that Gorvath's afterlife had grown soft with the unworthy, convinced the Host to turn on itself in a war of ascension he called the Final Proving. No faction achieved the victory it sought; the warring factions exhausted each other until none could hold their lands, and the territory of the Unbroken Host was lost to monsters and rival powers. No orc nation has reunified the Host's territory since.

### Modern Orcs

With the Unbroken Host centuries gone, most orcs today live in scattered holds, raiding companies, or as minorities within the cities of other peoples, rather than under any single orc banner. Gorvath's faith remains the most common thread tying these communities together, though its practice ranges from strict observance of the Forging Doctrine to little more than funeral custom. The argument Kharzug One-Eye once forced upon the whole Host, whether the Final Proving proved anything at all, still runs through the surviving sects, unresolved centuries later.

Like the other peoples once classed among Solvaris's bonded castes, orcs are today counted among the [[Freeborn]]. Few orcs lead with the term, however; Gorvath's faith and clan ties already give them a stronger sense of who they are, and "Freeborn" tends to matter more in dealings with outsiders than among orcs themselves.
