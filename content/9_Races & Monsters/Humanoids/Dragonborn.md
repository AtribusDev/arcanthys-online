The **Dragonborn** hold that every individual is born with potential, but potential alone is worthless. A life is judged not by birth, wealth, or rank, but by what one chooses to become. This belief forms the foundation of dragonborn society, producing a people who prize mastery, reputation, and personal sovereignty above all else.

Dragonborn are taught from a young age that every person should strive to become exceptional in a chosen calling. Whether that path is warfare, scholarship, craftsmanship, diplomacy, or farming is of little importance; what matters is the pursuit of excellence. A dragonborn who never commits themselves to mastering anything is viewed with quiet pity, having failed to realize their own potential.

## Physiology

Dragonborn are tall, powerfully built humanoids with a distinctly draconic bearing: scaled skin, clawed hands and feet, a long tail, and a reptilian head often marked by horns, frills, or a crest. They stand taller and heavier on average than humans, and their scales run in the same range of colors as [[True Dragons|true dragons]], from red, blue, green, black, and white to the metallic gold, silver, bronze, copper, and brass. A dragonborn's scale color marks their draconic ancestry and grants them a breath weapon and minor resistances matching that lineage, though it neither dictates their personality nor determines their morality.

## Culture

### Personal Sovereignty

Dragonborn believe every individual is sovereign over themselves, and every home is an extension of that sovereignty. This belief produces a culture that highly values independence, personal responsibility, and mutual respect. Authority is respected when earned through competence rather than inherited by birth, and masters of humble crafts are often afforded as much esteem as nobles or generals.

Hospitality is considered one of the highest expressions of this ideal. When a guest is welcomed into a dragonborn home, they are placed under the protection of the host. To betray or harm a guest is treated as an admission that the host cannot govern their own domain, bringing immense shame upon them.

Guests are expected to respect the sanctity of the home they enter in turn. Theft, deceit, or violence against a host are viewed as violations not merely of etiquette, but of the host's sovereignty.

### The Pursuit of Mastery

Upon reaching adulthood, most dragonborn declare a calling: a discipline to which they devote themselves. Individuals may change paths over a lifetime, but failing to pursue mastery of any craft at all is considered a life wasted.

Competition is common within dragonborn society, but rarely driven by envy or wealth. Instead, dragonborn compete to perfect their own skills, pushing one another toward greater achievement. Their cities are known for renowned artisans, scholars, soldiers, and builders whose ambition is to create works that outlast them. This pursuit of excellence has made dragonborn settlements famous for their architecture, craftsmanship, and institutions of learning.

### The Journey

It is customary for young dragonborn to leave their hometown for a time before fully establishing themselves. This journey is treated as an opportunity to test one's abilities, learn from foreign cultures, and discover what kind of person one wishes to become. Many dragonborn adventurers are simply following this tradition, seeking the experiences needed to define themselves before returning home, or choosing to build a new life abroad.

### Draconic Heritage

While all dragonborn share the same cultural ideals, their draconic ancestry shapes which qualities they are raised to admire most.

Scholars connect this to the same planar affinities that define [[True Dragons|true dragons]]: each color resonates most strongly with a plane whose temperament mirrors its own. Dragonborn do not carry this resonance directly, and their admired virtues are not a mirror of it. They are a disciplined, mortal-scale expression of the same underlying temperament. A black dragon's despair becomes, in a dragonborn, resilience; a white dragon's untempered aggression becomes directness. The color of one's scales marks which current runs closest beneath the skin, not what kind of person one is destined to become.

## Naming Customs

Dragonborn culture revolves around the concept of earning one's name. Unlike cultures where family or station grants prestige, a dragonborn's reputation is built through their own accomplishments. Children are known simply by their given names, but adults are typically identified by the deeds or craft for which they are respected.

A dragonborn may introduce themselves as:

> _"I am Rhogar, builder of the Stone Bridges."_
> _"I am Varesh, keeper of the Eastern Archives."_
> _"I am Sora, who broke the Siege of Khel."_

These are not formal titles, but descriptions of the accomplishments by which others know them. Among dragonborn, asking "Who are you?" is an invitation to speak of one's life's work, not one's occupation or lineage.

As a result, dragonborn place great importance on honesty about one's identity. Concealing one's name or accomplishments is seen as unusual, while falsely claiming another's deeds is among the gravest social disgraces.

## Relations with Other Peoples

Dragonborn often find other cultures difficult to understand. Societies built on inherited privilege can seem stagnant to them, while cultures that emphasize humility over achievement can seem content with mediocrity. Outsiders, in turn, sometimes mistake dragonborn pride for arrogance, failing to recognize that dragonborn generally admire excellence wherever it is found, regardless of social class or origin.

To a dragonborn, the measure of a person is simple: what have they devoted their life to becoming?
