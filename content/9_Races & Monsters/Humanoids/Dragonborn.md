The **Dragonborn** are a humanoid ([[Freeborn]]) people who hold that every individual is born with potential, but potential alone is worthless. A life is judged by what one chooses to become. Birth, wealth, and rank carry no weight in that judgment. This belief forms the foundation of dragonborn society, producing a people who prize mastery, reputation, and personal sovereignty above all else.

## Physiology

Dragonborn are tall, powerfully built humanoids with a distinctly draconic bearing: scaled skin, clawed hands and feet, a long tail, and a reptilian head often growing horns. They stand taller and heavier on average than humans, and their scales run in the same range of colors as [[True Dragons|true dragons]], from red, blue, green, black, and white to the metallic gold, silver, bronze, copper, and brass. A dragonborn's scale color marks their draconic ancestry and grants them a dragon's breath weapon and minor resistances matching that lineage, though it neither dictates their personality nor determines their morality.

## Personality

Dragonborn carry themselves with a confidence built on demonstrated skill. Birth and station carry no weight in it, and most take open pride in whatever mastery they have earned. Outsiders sometimes mistake this for arrogance, but a dragonborn generally reserves genuine pride for accomplishment itself and respects excellence wherever it appears, regardless of who displays it.

A dragonborn who never commits to mastering any craft is viewed with quiet pity by their peers.

## Culture

### Personal Sovereignty

Dragonborn believe every individual is sovereign over themselves, and every home is an extension of that sovereignty. This belief produces a culture that highly values independence, personal responsibility, and mutual respect. Dragonborn respect authority earned through competence. Inherited authority commands no such respect on its own, and masters of humble crafts are often afforded much esteem.

### The Pursuit of Mastery

Upon reaching adulthood, most dragonborn declare a calling, a discipline to which they devote themselves, whether warfare, scholarship, craftsmanship, diplomacy, or farming. What matters is the pursuit of excellence within it. Individuals may change paths over a lifetime, and it is rare to find a dragonborn without one.

Competition is common within dragonborn society. Dragonborn compete to perfect their own skills and push one another toward greater achievement, rarely out of envy or a desire for wealth.

### The Journey

It is customary for young dragonborn to leave their hometown for a time before fully establishing themselves. This journey is treated as an opportunity to test one's abilities, learn from foreign cultures, and discover what kind of person one wishes to become. Many dragonborn adventurers are following this tradition, seeking the experiences needed to define themselves before returning home.

### Hospitality

When a guest is welcomed into a dragonborn home, they are placed under the protection of the host. To betray or harm a guest is treated as an admission that the host cannot govern their own domain, bringing immense shame upon them.

Guests are expected to respect the sanctity of the home they enter in turn. Theft, deceit, or violence against a host are treated as more than a breach of etiquette. They are violations of the host's sovereignty itself.

### Draconic Heritage

A dragonborn's draconic heritage extends to the planar affinities of [[True Dragons]], and each heritage resonates strongly with the plane it is drawn to, shaping the virtues that lineage tends to admire. A dragonborn with a blue heritage is likely to admire order and leadership, while one with a silver heritage might admire community and mentorship.

When a plane's influence grows strong enough, it can affect dragonborn of a matching heritage, infusing them with the plane's essence and potentially altering their physiology and mind alike.

## Naming Customs

Dragonborn culture revolves around the concept of earning one's name: a dragonborn's reputation is built entirely through their own accomplishments. Family or station grants no prestige on its own. Children are known simply by their given names, but adults are typically identified by the deeds or craft for which they are respected.

A dragonborn may introduce themselves as:

> _"I am Rhogar, builder of the Stone Bridges."_
> _"I am Varesh, keeper of the Eastern Archives."_
> _"I am Sora, who broke the Siege of Khel."_

These carry no weight as formal titles. They are descriptions of the accomplishments by which others know that dragonborn. Among dragonborn, asking "Who are you?" is an invitation to speak of one's life's work rather than one's occupation or lineage.

As a result, dragonborn place great importance on honesty about one's identity. Concealing one's name or accomplishments is seen as unusual.

## Relations with Other Peoples

Dragonborn often find other cultures difficult to understand. Societies built on inherited privilege can seem stagnant to them, while cultures that emphasize humility over achievement can seem content with mediocrity. Outsiders, in turn, sometimes mistake dragonborn pride for arrogance, failing to recognize that dragonborn generally admire excellence wherever it is found, regardless of social class or origin.
