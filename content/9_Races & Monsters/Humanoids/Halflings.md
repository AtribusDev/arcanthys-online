**Halflings** are a humanoid ([[Freeborn]]) people of Arcanthys, small in stature, named by other peoples for standing roughly half the height of an average human. Other peoples commonly call them the Good Folk, a byname earned by their even temperament content with hearth, food, and good company over ambition, land, or title.

## Physiology

Halflings stand small, rarely more than three and a half feet tall. Build and coloring vary about as widely as a human's but rarely draw a second glance either way: hair in ordinary browns, blacks, and reds, skin across the same range of tones common among humans.

Halfling hair is commonly worn long and elaborately, gathered into complex braids or woven strands; the men among them are almost universally unable to grow a true beard, and many wear long sideburns in its place.

## Personality

Halflings are cheerful and easy company: quick to laugh, quicker to make a stranger feel welcome, and rarely troubled for long by anything short of real hardship. That same easy manner serves a practical purpose. Halflings live every day among peoples taller, stronger, and often more dangerous than themselves, and a friendly, unassuming bearing keeps a halfling out of notice and out of danger more reliably than physical force could; few halflings find this a hardship, and most would rather be underestimated than feared.

A halfling's mind runs practical, not grand, taking genuine pleasure in small, immediate things: a good meal, a warm fire, a well-told story. Halflings are frequently curious to a fault and would rather court a bit of trouble than sit through a dull afternoon, and those who do take up adventuring rarely lack for courage once trouble finds them.

Many halflings also carry a marked fondness for drink and well-made possessions, collecting keepsakes and small treasures with the same enthusiasm they bring to a good meal. A halfling home is rarely bare, however plain its owner's ambitions otherwise run.

### The Unorthodox Few

Not every halfling fits the Good Folk moniker. A small number stand as a near complete inversion of the typical halfling temperament: sadistic, bloodthirsty, and greedy, finding employment as rogues, enforcers, and other shady trades where none would expect to find a halfling at all. Their vicious reputation earned them another name, [[Bloodlings|bloodlings]], used to mark the distinction.

Bloodlings are closely associated with the [[The Fell Lords|Fell Lord]] [[Kaldrus, The Chain Eternal|Kaldrus]]. They first drew wide notice as the core membership of the [[Cult of the Chain]], whose members broke the seal binding Kaldrus and loosed the Fell Lord to wreak havoc on [[Kaelvarra]]. Bloodlings are believed to still make up a significant share of the cult's membership today.

## Culture

Halfling communities value a good hearth, reliable food, and people worth knowing above land, standing, or ambition. They describe that as a complete life, not a consolation for a bigger one denied. That contentment comes with a marked aloofness toward matters outside it: halflings tend not to involve themselves in the politics, feuds, or grand ambitions of other peoples, and see little reason to start. A halfling caught angling for a neighbor's land, a guildmaster's chair, or any other mark of standing draws notice precisely because it is unusual.

Most halflings want exactly what they have. The temperament passes from parent to child as reliably as height or coloring.

## Habitat

Halflings rarely settle lands of their own, and few halfling communities stand as settlements in their own right. Instead, most halflings take up a single quarter or a scattering of streets within a larger town or city raised by another people, living in close quarters with their hosts and taking up their neighbors' rhythms of daily life while still keeping their own customs at home. This arrangement suits the temperament that earns them their byname: a Good Folk community asks little of its neighbors beyond room enough for a hearth, and gives back undemanding company in return.

These quarters cluster mostly across former Dominion territory, in the same towns and cities where halfling communities first took root, though a Good Folk enclave can be found in nearly any settlement willing to have one.

## Relations with Other Peoples

Other Freeborn peoples tend to regard halflings with a mix of genuine fondness and reflexive underestimation, an impression halflings do little to correct and, if anything, find mildly funny. Most communities welcome a halfling quarter, and halflings return the sentiment by keeping to their own hearths.

## History

### Current Age (Age of Wonders)

Halflings have no creation myth of their own and no known history before the [[The Dominion of Solvaris|Dominion of Solvaris]]. They appear in Dominion records as an already-established population beginning around [[TODO, YEARS]]. Rumor holds that they were first encountered during Solvarian excursions into [[Vaeluun]] and brought back into the Dominion from there, though no surviving record confirms it. Halflings themselves have no preserved memory of a homeland nor any ambition to return to it.

Solvarian households took to halflings quickly. Their timid, unassuming manner made them popular servants, and within a few generations halflings were a common sight in estates and farmsteads across Dominion territory. They were treated with a moderate degree of respect compared to other subjugated peoples: still compelled into service, but assigned jobs and given compensation suited to their temperament.

### The Second Dawn and After

Halfling servitude ended without incident. No campaign was fought over it, and no record marks the date it stopped in any given household; halflings took up the [[Freeborn]] identity alongside the rest of the Dominion's former bonded castes after [[The Second Dawn]] and began living as they saw fit. Most stayed close to the households and towns they already knew, settling into quarters and streets within other peoples' settlements across former Dominion territory, valuing hearth, food, and good company over land, title, or reputation.

#### The Cult of the Chain

Around [[TODO, YEAR]] halflings found themselves at the center of a strange incident. The [[Cult of the Chain]], a previously unknown cult that broke the seal binding [[Kaldrus, The Chain Eternal|Kaldrus]], drew an unusually large share of its membership from among halflings. These halflings became known as bloodlings for their sadistic characters. Kaldrus was sealed again, and the cult was broken up with it.
