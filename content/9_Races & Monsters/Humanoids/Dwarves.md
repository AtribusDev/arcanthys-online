**Dwarves** are a humanoid ([[Freeborn]]) people of Arcanthys, known across the world for carving whole cities from stone and for a martial tradition, the [[Stonewatch]], that traces its purpose back to the prisons built to hold the [[The Fell Lords|Fell Lords]].

Most dwarves today do not take up that watch, and not every one of the old prisons still has a clan standing over it. The duty still runs through dwarven culture regardless: dwarves build to last, dig deep, and do not open their trust to outsiders quickly or often.

## Physiology

Dwarves are short and powerfully built, dense enough that a dwarf is typically far heavier than their height would suggest to those unfamiliar with the people. Skin runs to pale, weathered tones across most clans. Hair and beards grow thick and are worn long by both sexes, often bound with clasps and rings of worked metal.

Dwarves also carry a marked resistance to poison, an inheritance of their stone origin: legends say the first dwarves were shaped whole from stone rather than flesh, and stone does not sicken or choke the way flesh does. Generations of mining, one of the more lethal trades wherever it is practiced and one that exposes workers to foul air, packed dust, and gas seeping from freshly cut seams, have only reinforced it since.

## Personality

Dwarves extend trust slowly and expect the same caution from others. A stranger, dwarf or otherwise, is treated with polite distance until they have proven themselves through repeated, consistent conduct. A single good deed is not enough. Once trust is given in full, it is given completely. A dwarf who calls someone friend means it as an unconditional, lifelong claim. It is never offered as a passing courtesy.

The same holds true in reverse. A debt unpaid, a trust broken, a duty abandoned are not forgotten, and time does not erase them. A dwarven grudge outlives the dwarf who first held it, passing down the clan like a craft, or a family blade, and to outsiders this can be dangerous: a grudge kept for generations is also a grudge no one living can forgive. A dwarf's temper works the same way as their trust, slow to give, and just as slow to cool.

## Culture

### Clans and Elders

Dwarven society is organized by clan before anything else, and a clan's authority rests with its eldest members rather than its strongest or wealthiest. Major decisions are brought before a council of elders, and a ruling reached this way is expected to hold even by those who argued against it. A council weighing a major matter often looks to how the clan's own ancestors met a similar matter before.

This deference to age extends beyond dwarven elders. A dwarf extends the same basic respect to the elders of other peoples, on the reasoning that long life is proof enough of hard-won judgment wherever it is found.

Clasps and rings of worked metal are gifted by an elder or clan on the completion of a true work or the swearing of an oath, and worn thereafter as marks of that honor.

### Ancestors

History, and particularly the deeds of past ancestors, occupies a central place in dwarven life. An ancestor's great deeds are often remembered and celebrated over drink and food. All dwarves aspire to become ancestors worth remembering.

### The Stonewatch

Each dwarf clan traces itself back to a [[Wardholds|wardhold]], a fortress hold built with the help of the [[Elder Gods]] to hold a [[The Fell Lords|Fell Lord]]. The dwarves fulfilling that duty are known collectively as the [[Stonewatch]]. Most dwarves alive now are not Stonewatch, tracing their clan back to a wardhold lost generations before. Some were lost to assaults or corruption but most were lost during the [[Age of the Deep]] when the ocean flooded most caverns. A clan without a wardhold rarely lets the matter rest, reclaiming a lost wardhold is counted among the highest honors a clan member can still pursue.

### Industry and Infrastructure

Dwarven life centers on work adapted to the underground: mining, stonework, smelting, and forging. Dwarven cities are built entirely around the infrastructure that supports this industry, with minecarts, vertical lifts, and waterways sprawling through the cities and the caverns around them.

Dwarves have become masters of manipulating water. They alter waterways to move trees, charcoal, and trade goods between the surface and the depths, drain caverns flooded by [[Uru’gol, The Open Maw|Uru’gol]]'s Drowned Spawn, and power waterwheels that drive their forges and smelters.

## Habitat

### Holds Bored Into Stone

Dwarves make their homes underground, in [[Wardholds|wardholds]] and other holds bored into mountains and deep rock across Arcanthys. A hold grows outward from the rock it occupies, chamber and hall cut out over decades. Dwarven industry sprawls through the caverns surrounding each hold. Dwarven cities are slow to build but are made to last.

## Naming Customs

### Names Given and Earned

A typical dwarf carries a birth name and a clan name for life. A third name, a hearth-name, can be earned. A hearth-name echoes the clan name closely. A hearth-name is rarely given. It is proof that an individual has proven themselves a devoted member or friend of the clan. A dwarf who holds a hearth-name leads with birth name and hearth-name, dropping the clan name from how they are addressed. All hearth-names a clan has given are recorded in a ledger that is treated as a near-sacred document and recited often.

## Relations with Other Peoples

Most dwarves prefer to stay within the bounds of their underground cities. They have little care for the culture of others but are not unwelcoming of outsiders. Though strangers may perceive the dwarves as distantly indifferent, anyone willing to put in the effort can find great, lifelong friends among them.

The nature of dwarves means they struggle to connect with nations or factions that wronged them in the past, even when those nations or factions are now represented by new faces. Dwarven allegiances rarely shift, making them fierce enemies and reliable allies.

## History

### Age of Shaping

Legends say dwarves were shaped during the [[Age of Shaping]] by [[Baugrun, The Unsound|Baugrun]], now counted among the [[The Fell Lords|Fell Lords]], from its own stone body. Baugrun served, for a time, as the first and only elder any dwarf answered to, and dwarven legends remember that age fondly, an elder who taught the first dwarves to read stone and to work it to outlast themselves.

According to legend, the near parental bond between dwarf and god ended when war broke out between the [[The First Gods|First Gods]] and the Fell Lords. Baugrun stood with the First Gods at first, and set its own people to driving new seams into the earth for the ore and stone the war demanded. It was in that digging that the dwarves cut into something that no dwarf had found before: the [[Worldcore]]. Some legends call it a single stone unlike any before or since, others a seam without an end. Whatever it was, it took hold of Baugrun completely, and its want for it hardened into something larger than Baugrun's love for its own dwarves.

Legend holds that when the dwarves confronted it over the change, Baugrun turned on them rather than answer for it. It declared that dwarvenkind owed it service for everything it had shaped and taught them. It broke with the First Gods that day and went over to the Fell Lords. Dwarvenkind refused and held to the First Gods to the war's end. Baugrun's betrayal is counted as dwarvenkind's first and oldest grudge.

The same legends hold that, having sealed the surviving Fell Lords away, the [[The First Gods|First Gods]] turned to dwarvenkind, who still bore the grudge against Baugrun, and asked them to help guard the seals. To answer that call, the dwarves divided into the many clans that endure to this day, each raising its own wardhold above one of the sealed remnants of the Age of Shaping. Together, these clans became known as the Stonewatch. The First Gods ensured that no clan was ever told which Fell Lord lay beneath its wardhold, so no dwarf would knowingly stand watch over its own sealed elder.

### Age of the Baneful

As the [[Kelthrae]] reshaped the surface world during the [[Age of the Baneful]], twisting land and life into new and often hostile forms, dwarven holds counted among the few places able to resist that influence. The wardholds that endured the age did so mostly untouched by Kelthrae corruption, cut deep enough that the transformations sweeping the surface never reached their halls.

### Age of the Deep

When the seas rose during the [[Age of the Deep]] and Uru'gol's Drowned Spawn began emerging from flooded caverns and buried rivers far inland, the dwarven clans suffered greatly. Entire wardholds were lost as garrisons, built to guard sealed prisons, were overwhelmed by the floods. Every hold was defended until it was completely flooded, yet most were lost all the same.

### Current Age (Age of Wonders)

#### Under the Dominion

Dwarves were counted among the [[The Dominion of Solvaris|Dominion of Solvaris]]'s subject peoples, yet they were granted greater autonomy than most. Solvarian interest in dwarves centered on their craftsmanship and their knowledge of the deep earth, and they were entrusted with fueling much of the Dominion's industry. As a result, most holds and dwarven traditions from this period remain much as they were before the Dominion's rule.

#### The Second Dawn and Modern Dwarves

Dwarves embraced the Freeborn identity after [[The Second Dawn]]. Most dwarves today live much as their ancestors always did, mining, smithing, and carving new chambers from old stone, with no active Stonewatch duty.

Yet Uru'gol's Drowned Spawn remain a constant threat, and not only to the Stonewatch clans. A hold breaking new ground may still occasionally breach a flooded seam grown around a Lesser Maw, most of which are small and long dormant, but no less dangerous for it. Every dwarven miner is trained to recognize the warning signs.
