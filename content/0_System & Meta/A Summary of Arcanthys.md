**Arcanthys** is a world where life and magic share a single source, where an empire's ambition once nearly burned both away, and where every convenience born of magic still carries the risk of its own collapse.

Magic is not rare in Arcanthys. It lights streets, powers engines, drives industry, and shapes daily life. That convenience exists alongside deep instability. The same energy that heals, builds, and illuminates can also warp flesh, corrupt landscapes, and unmake entire cities.

Long ago, the Dominion of Solvaris rose to rule most of the known world through magical industry. Its fall in a catastrophe now called the Second Dawn shattered political order, scarred the land, and changed how people understand both magic and the gods. Every nation, cult, guild, and family in the present age still lives in its shadow.

## The Core Truth of the World

All life in Arcanthys is sustained by a radiant essence called [[Lumir]], which flows outward from the sun, [[Arcanum (Sun)|Arcanum]], through the [[Material Plane]] and into every living thing. Most people never study Lumir directly, but everyone depends on it.

As a result, life in Arcanthys holds two conflicting realities: magical technology makes ordinary life more efficient, safer, and more connected, while overuse, mishandling, or destabilization of that same magic can produce disasters that spread faster than any army. Progress is real. So is fear.

## Magic and Technology in Everyday Life

Arcanthys runs on [[Lumitech]]: engineered devices and infrastructure that replicate magical effects through machinery rather than requiring a spellcaster every time. Cities can run on magical lighting and automated systems, transport and industry rely on lumitech networks, and most people benefit from magic daily even if they cannot cast a spell themselves.

To make this system scalable, Lumir is often refined into [[Mana]], a dense, condensed fuel suited to storage and high-demand systems. Mana made modern infrastructure possible, but it introduced new failure points: damaged or poorly contained Mana can violently discharge its stored energy, with the risk of [[Mageblight]] close behind.

Trained spellcasters still exist, but they are uncommon compared to the scale of magical industry. Everyday society is built less around archmages and more around artificers, engineers, and maintenance crews who keep unstable systems functioning.

Lumitech's reach is uneven across the world. The continent of [[Kaelvarra]] holds the densest concentration of advanced lumitech infrastructure, and much of the rest of the world remains lower-tech, where travel, warfare, and daily life look far closer to traditional fantasy norms. In campaign terms, this usually means urban arcane industry and factional intrigue in Kaelvarra, and more classic frontier or kingdom-scale adventures elsewhere.

## The Great Risk: Mageblight

When magical systems collapse or overload, Lumir can turn corrosive and self-propagating. This is [[Mageblight]]: blue-violet, flesh-like growths threaded with glowing veins, spreading through walls, pipes, and machinery. Left untreated, minor contamination can mature into reactive, aggressive growths that consume entire districts and, at its worst, reshape whole landscapes, as it did in creating the crystalline [[Glass Wastes]] and the colorless [[Bleached Lands]].

Most people treat minor blight like industrial rot or toxic runoff: unpleasant, dangerous, but familiar. That attitude keeps society functioning. It also means small failures are often ignored until they become large ones.

## The Dominion of Solvaris and the Second Dawn

The [[The Dominion of Solvaris|Dominion of Solvaris]] began as a colony of exiles driven from [[Talorin]] to the hostile continent of [[Solkarra]]. Under the founder [[Aethren Solivar Draal]], the survivors developed the earliest Lumitech and used it to secure their new home, uniting under a Governing Council rather than a crown. In the centuries that followed, that same council turned Solvaris into the most powerful state of its era: militarized, industrial, and built on large-scale magical engineering, its methods reshaping warfare, infrastructure, and governance across most of the known world. Public worship of the gods was outlawed within its borders, and non-human peoples lived as members of legally "bonded castes" denied the rights held by human citizens.

Then came the Second Dawn. Witnesses describe a second sun appearing over the Solvarian capital, followed by a global surge of Lumir. Lumitech systems failed at scale, and entire populations died in moments to the [[Fleshfire]], which caused victims to ignite from within as their own Lumir overloaded catastrophically. The capital was reduced to the Glass Wastes, the Dominion's institutions fractured, and its territory collapsed into succession struggles and ideological conflict. Many attribute the disaster to the Dominion's own unprecedented scale of Lumitech use, though its precise cause remains debated. The world that followed is not post-magic. It is post-certainty.

In the Dominion's fall, the peoples it had once bonded found a new, shared identity: [[Freeborn]], a term claimed by elves, dwarves, orcs, and every other people the Dominion had once sorted beneath human citizens. It is a political and historical identity rather than an ethnic one, and it says plainly that no bond holds any longer.

## Historical Context Beyond Solvaris

Solvaris is the single most important event in recent history, but it is not the beginning of history. Its fall in the Second Dawn occurred 700 years ago, and the present age is still measured from that rupture. Its rise as an imperial power, by contrast, began roughly 2,700 years ago, after several far older ages had already shaped the world.

**The Age of Shaping:** According to the oldest surviving legend, [[Arcanum (Creator God of Myth)|Arcanum]] gave the young world its order and charged the [[The First Gods|First Gods]] to shape it, filling it with beast, root, and season. Other gods followed, in time remembered as the [[The Fell Lords|Fell Lords]], and shaped mortals able to think, feel, and choose for themselves. The two sides could not agree on what these new mortals were meant to be, and went to war over them. Before that war reached its end, three Fell Lords, [[Kharox]], [[Myzra]], and [[Threxul]], were drawn to their deaths within the newly formed [[The Everdeep|Everdeep]] by a First God, [[Vhalis]], in a clash said to have given rise to the first [[Celestials]] and [[Fiends]]. The four are remembered together as the [[The Lost Deities|Lost Deities]], the only gods known to have perished without return. The war itself is said to have ended only when mortal belief lent the First Gods a strength no god had wielded before, binding the surviving Fell Lords within Arcanthys and leading the First Gods to withdraw from the world they had saved.

**The Age of the Baneful:** With the gods gone, [[The Kelthrae|the Kelthrae]] descended and twisted the life the gods had made into new and monstrous forms. Dragons struck an uneasy pact with the Fell Lord [[Uru’gol, The Open Maw|Uru'gol]], only to betray him and their kraken allies once corrupted by the Kelthrae's influence. The krakens freed Uru'gol in retaliation, and the seas rose across Arcanthys.

**The Age of the Deep:** For most of this era, much of Arcanthys lay beneath the sea, and kraken dominion under Uru'gol shaped a flooded world. The age ended when Uru'gol was bound again and the waters receded, though not to their former lines.

**The Age of Orcs:** As the waters withdrew, the first great humanoid civilizations rose across the reopened lands, chief among them the Unbroken Host, a vast orc civilization built from once-nomadic peoples. It collapsed under its own civil war, and its territories fragmented and were absorbed, contested, or overrun by what followed.

By the time Solvaris emerged, Arcanthys already had layered myths, ruined polities, old religious systems, and inherited traumas. Solvaris did not create history. It reorganized, weaponized, and then shattered a world that was already ancient.

## The Present Age

No power has fully replaced Solvaris. The current age, sometimes called the Age of Wonders, is defined by uneven recovery, local power blocs, and competing definitions of stability.

Common conditions across regions include Solvarian ruins treated as both strategic assets and active hazards, political struggle over legacy infrastructure and fuel supply chains, ongoing tension between technocratic institutions and older religious authorities, and a widespread fear that mismanaged Lumitech could trigger another civilization-scale disaster. The world is rebuilding, but there is no consensus on what responsible progress looks like.

## What Most Characters Know

Even without formal education, most people in Arcanthys know a few practical truths: Solvaris fell 700 years ago, and its ruins are dangerous as often as they are valuable; Lumitech is useful and common in many places, but far from equally developed across the world; Mageblight is a known risk, and small signs of it are often ignored until problems escalate; faith and politics are deeply linked; and no one agrees on how far magical progress should go, but everyone has to live with its consequences.

## Gods, Faith, and Divine Power

The gods of Arcanthys are real, but distant. They rarely act directly, and their influence comes through signs, doctrine, chosen servants, and the institutions built around worship.

The [[Elder Gods]] are a varied collection of deities tied to particular peoples and cultures that predate Solvaris, such as the orc war-god [[Gorvath Iron-Blood, the First Victor|Gorvath Iron-Blood]]. Their worship tends to be deeply rooted in local tradition, ritual, and identity.

The [[The Arcanarchs|Arcanarchs]] are gods recognized in the wake of Solvaris's fall, each tied to an aspect of the world the present age most needs, such as [[Vhal]], the Arcanarch of Protection and Vigilance, whose worship runs through the Wardens' and Shieldbearers' Guilds by way of the Vigil Covenant. Arcanarch worship regularly fuses faith with labor and industry: a workshop can function as a shrine, and loyalty to a god can determine access to power grids, medicine, or supply.

Standing apart from both is [[The Guide]], the most recently acknowledged deity, a god of magic and knowledge with no fixed form, who speaks directly and rarely to spellcasters and occasionally marks the most promising among them with a guidebrand.

The [[The Fell Lords|Fell Lords]] are the strongest of fiends, demi-gods who ruled parts of Arcanthys during the Age of Shaping before being sealed away, each said to embody a particular mortal fear. They are not worshipped as ordinary gods so much as feared as bound catastrophes. When a Fell Lord's bindings fail, as Uru'gol's have twice, large-scale calamity follows. Not every Fell Lord survived to be bound: three died in the war's final clashes, along with the First God who opposed them, and are remembered as the [[The Lost Deities|Lost Deities]].

Faith is never separate from politics in Arcanthys. Temples control resources, priesthoods shape law, and guilds adopt religious identities of their own.

## Planes and the Soul

Arcanthys exists within a larger planar system. Nine outer planes orbit the Material Plane, each sustained by a particular emotional current expressed by mortal life: [[Sylvara, The Feywild|Sylvara]] by joy and curiosity, [[Noctyra, The Shadowfell|Noctyra]] by grief and despair, [[Avereth, The Overbright|Avereth]] by hope, [[Varynth, The Rising Labyrinth|Varynth]] by ambition, [[Thaloria, The Unbound Wilds|Thaloria]] by instinct and freedom, [[Gildara, The Boundless Embrace|Gildara]] by love and compassion, and [[Rathune, The Crimson Expanse|Rathune]] by anger. When a plane's currents strengthen, its influence over the Material Plane grows more pronounced.

Souls are said to originate as Arcanum's radiance given consciousness, descending into the Material Plane to bind with the living. At death, a soul drifts toward Noctyra and is gradually drawn into the Everdeep, a vast convergence of the dead at that plane's heart, where identity erodes as it merges into the greater whole. Resurrection is possible only while enough of the soul remains distinct from that convergence, and grows costlier and less certain the longer a soul has been lost within it. What becomes of a soul beyond that point, whether it returns to Arcanum to be given form again or simply ceases, is not something common belief agrees on.

## What Makes Arcanthys a Campaign World

Arcanthys is built for stories about consequence. Discovery can save a city or doom it. Faith can be sincere, political, manipulative, or all three at once. Ancient ruins are not just treasure vaults, but unresolved systems still affecting the present. Moral choices are rarely clean, because every faction is trying to prevent one disaster by risking another.

Heroes in Arcanthys do not choose between magic and no magic. They choose what kind of future magic will create, who controls it, and who pays for it. That is the central question of the world: not whether power exists, but whether anyone can wield it without repeating the fall of Solvaris.
