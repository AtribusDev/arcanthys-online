**The Dominion of Solvaris** was the largest nation on [[Arcanthys]] during the [[Current Age]], composed predominantly of [[Humans|humans]]. It maintained a strong focus on military power and arcane research, particularly [[Lumitech]], which allowed it to gain control of most of the known world.

# Population
At its height, the Dominion of Solvaris is estimated to have had a population of X million inhabitants. Humans comprised the ruling class and primary citizenry and were called Solvarites. For every human citizen, eight non-humans lived as members of the bonded castes, denied the rights afforded to humans under Solvarian law.

Solvarite humans are generally tall and broad-shouldered, with strong features and upright bearing. They are often noted for their disciplined posture, measured speech, and an air of practiced authority that many outside Solvaris find imposing. Most have dark hair in shades of black or deep brown, though bronze, golden, or pale blond hair appears among certain old bloodlines and is traditionally associated with noble descent. Children born with such features are often raised with great expectation and instructed in matters of state, war, or faith from an early age. Solvarites favor well-kept hair worn short or bound back, reflecting their cultural preference for order, uniformity, and practicality.

# Culture
Solvarian culture values adaptation, strength, and order in all areas of life. These ideals were shaped by the harsh conditions of Solvaris, where the first settlements arose upon a large and resource-rich island beset by constant danger. Though the land held valuable materials, it offered little arable ground, and its interior was home to numerous monstrous threats. The surrounding seas were equally perilous, isolating early communities and making travel uncertain.

In response to these conditions, the Solvari developed a society that prized discipline, ingenuity, and collective purpose. Scarcity encouraged efficiency, while constant danger fostered martial traditions and strict social organization. Necessity also drove innovation. By adapting the resources available to them, Solvarian artificers and scholars created the earliest forms of lumitech, blending arcane principles with practical engineering.

With these advances, the people of Solvaris secured their homeland and overcame those who had once threatened them. In later centuries, however, the same institutions and technologies that had ensured their survival became the foundation of imperial expansion. What began as a culture forged in resistance gradually transformed into one that sought to impose its order upon others.

Solvarian culture often regarded non-humans as lesser: races with physical or magical advantages over humans were seen as relying too heavily on those strengths, lacking the wisdom to embrace technology and adapt. Most non-humans were thralls or members of lower society, treated with disrespect and denied the rights afforded to humans. Solvarian culture was comparatively fair to those non-humans who embraced its ways and proved useful to the dominion, granting such individuals greater rights than others of their kind.

## A land of exiles
The history of the Dominion of Solvaris begins with the exile of its earliest ancestors from [[Talorin]] to the distant continent of [[Solkarra]], then known as Tazora. [[TODO, BANISHMENT REASON, a church run nation sounds cool]]

Tazora was a hostile and untamed continent. Poisoned marshes, broken highlands, and dense wildlands were inhabited by stirges, trolls, giant insects, and numerous monstrosities. Nations avoided the island entirely, owing to the influence of [[The Thirteen Sisters]], a powerful hag coven feeding off a potent confluence with [[Sylvara, The Feywild|The Feywild]].

The exiles survived only in scattered enclaves along the coast and outer reaches of the continent. These early settlements were isolated, poorly supplied, and under constant threat from both the land and its rulers. The exiles prayed to each and every god but none ever intervened.

The arrival of [[Aethren Solivar Draal]] marked a decisive turning point. Accounts differ as to the details of the founder’s life before exile, but most agree that he possessed considerable arcane talent and had concealed it in order to avoid lethal persecution. Condemned to Tazora, he began studying unusual native materials that demonstrated an exceptional affinity for the arcane.

From this research came the earliest forms of Lumitech. These implements provided the exiles with weapons, tools, and defensive capabilities previously beyond their means. Under the founder’s leadership, the scattered enclaves entered into common cause and began a proper conflict with The Thirteen Sisters. The coven commanded monsters, curses, and deep knowledge of the land, while the exiles relied on discipline, innovation, and necessity.

The decisive act of the war was the construction of a powerful Lumitech engine designed to sever Tazora’s connection to The Feywild. When this link was broken, the strength of the coven diminished substantially, allowing the Sisters to be slain, scattered, or driven into hiding.

## The First Solvarian State
With the defeat of the coven, [[Aethren Solivar Draal]] united the surviving settlements and established the first Solvarian state. Rather than rule as a sole monarch, he founded a governing council, drawn from the leaders of the formerly scattered enclaves, to administer the new state. This council, not a hereditary crown, remained Solvaris's supreme political authority for the remainder of its history.

The generations that followed saw rapid transformation across Tazora. Arcane engines were used to reclaim farmland, secure roads, mechanize fisheries, fortify settlements, and tame regions once considered uninhabitable.

During this period, several values came to define Solvarian society. Strength was celebrated in many forms, including martial discipline, scholarship, magical ability, and civic service. Advancement was tied more closely to merit than lineage, particularly when compared to many contemporary kingdoms. At the same time, the memory of abandonment in exile and the perceived complicity of priesthoods in that suffering gave rise to a deeply rooted anti-theistic tradition. Solvarian philosophers argued that no divine power had saved them, and that no mortal institution should claim authority through the favor of the gods.

As Solvaris prospered, its ambitions expanded beyond survival and reconstruction. State resources increasingly shifted toward military production, and Lumitech innovation turned toward engines of war. Old grievances against [[Talorin]] and other nations of the known world were cultivated alongside a growing belief that Solvarian order and progress ought to replace the fractured systems beyond their shores.

## The Dominion of Solvaris
In **2000 BA**, Solvarian airships appeared over [[Talorin]], marking the first large-scale use of modern aerial warfare. Arcane bombardments and rapid deployments rendered many traditional defenses ineffective, and several major powers fell in quick succession. Historians generally identify this campaign as the beginning of the Dominion of Solvaris as an imperial power.

Expansion in the centuries that followed was swift. Under the Governing Council's direction, conquered territories were incorporated into an increasingly centralized imperial system. Local laws were replaced or subordinated to Dominion authority, while infrastructure, industry, and military administration were reorganized along Solvarian lines. In many regions, talented individuals of any ancestry could rise through service to the state, a policy that distinguished the Dominion from more hereditary rivals.

The most controversial element of Solvarian rule was its treatment of religion. Public worship of the gods was prohibited across much of the empire, temples were closed or repurposed, and established priesthoods were stripped of legal authority. Official doctrine framed these measures as liberation from institutions that had historically empowered tyrants and divided peoples.

The contradiction between Solvarian ideals and provincial realities became more pronounced over time. While the imperial heartlands promoted merit, order, and rational governance, distant territories were frequently shaped by military rule, exploitation, and ethnic hierarchy imposed by local governors or collaborators.

## The Fall of Solvaris
In 0 AA, the Dominion ended with [[The Second Dawn]]: the sudden appearance of a second sun above the Solvarian capital and the wave of destructive Lumir it released. The capital was reduced to what is now known as the [[Glass Wastes]] within a single day, and the Dominion's imperial administration and military collapsed along with it. Its former territories broke apart into successor states and contested regions, marking the end of Solvarian rule and the beginning of the [[Current Age]].

The precise cause of the Second Dawn remains debated, though many attribute it to the Dominion's own unprecedented scale of lumitech use.
