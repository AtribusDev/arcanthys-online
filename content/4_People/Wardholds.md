**A wardhold** is a dwarven fortress-hold built to guard one of the deep prisons sealing a [[The Fell Lords|Fell Lord]], the founding seat of a dwarf clan, and the origin point of the [[Stonewatch]], the clans still keeping that duty today.

## Founding

The first wardholds were raised during the [[Age of Shaping]], in the same span that dwarvenkind, having just judged and struck [[Baugrun, The Unsound|Baugrun]] for its betrayal, split from one people under one elder into many smaller clans. Each clan took up a wardhold of its own: a hold built specifically over one sealed remnant of the war against [[The Fell Lords|the Fell Lords]], Baugrun's own prison foremost and oldest among them. Dwarven tradition holds that the [[The First Gods|First Gods]] lent their own hand in raising the wardholds, and saw to it that no clan was ever told which Fell Lord its own wardhold held, so that what a clan does not know can never be tricked or tortured out of it.

Every dwarven clan alive today traces its founding, however dimly, back to one of these first wardholds, whether or not the clan still remembers what its founders were originally set to watch. Most no longer do.

## Construction and Wardwork

A wardhold is warded as deliberately as it is built to last, carved through with warding patterns meant to hold a sealed prison closed and, where the danger is understood, to withstand slow subsidence and sudden shaking alike. A wardhold's deepest wardwork is generally its oldest and most reinforced, kept and renewed by the clan across generations rather than raised once and left standing.

## Loss and Reclamation

Not every wardhold raised at the founding of the clans is still garrisoned. A line dying out, a prison proving stable enough to need no further attention, or a clan simply drifting into ordinary mining over enough generations has left most dwarves several steps removed from any active watch. A clan without a wardhold of its own rarely lets the matter rest: reclaiming a lost wardhold is counted among the highest honors a clan can still pursue, whatever mining and smithing fill its days in the meantime.

## The Age of the Deep

When the seas rose during the [[Age of the Deep]] and [[Uru’gol, The Open Maw|Uru’gol]]'s Drowned Spawn began surfacing in flooded caverns and buried rivers far inland, wardholds were among the first casualties: garrisons built to guard a sealed prison were overrun instead by a Lesser Maw taking root in their lower halls, the flood proving a more immediate danger than whatever each hold had been built to contain. It is this era, and the wardholds lost to Uru'gol's flood specifically, that dwarven tradition points to as the moment the scattered, hold-by-hold practice of the founding clans became the coordinated Stonewatch dwarves maintain today.

## Under the Dominion

Under the [[The Dominion of Solvaris|Dominion of Solvaris]], Dominion engineers pressed dwarven miners into digging toward mineral veins and lumitech materials that lay near or through old wardholds, and at least a few clans, under threat or promise of better treatment, complied. The breaches this caused were usually minor and were sealed again in time, but the clans responsible are still named plainly in dwarven memory.

## Modern Wardholds

Where a clan's wardhold still stands and its watch continues, the duty is maintained quietly and without fanfare, its details shared only with those the clan has fully accepted. A hold breaking new ground, wardhold or otherwise, still occasionally cuts into a flooded seam grown up around a Lesser Maw. Sealing the seam, abandoning it outright, or sending word to a Stonewatch clan better equipped to deal with it is a decision most dwarven holds have faced at least once within living memory.
