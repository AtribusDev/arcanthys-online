**Mageblight** is a form of uncontrolled Lumiric corruption caused by the collapse, overload, or destabilization of magic. Though most commonly associated with failed spellcasting, Mageblight can also emerge from damaged, neglected, or improperly maintained [[Lumitech|lumitech]].

# Origin
No one knows for certain when Mageblight first appeared, or whether it has an origin at all. Some hold that it has always accompanied magic in Arcanthys, an inherent hazard of drawing on lumir that predates any recorded age. Others argue it is a more recent affliction, though none can point to a period in which magic was demonstrably free of it.

What is settled is that Mageblight has grown far more common and severe since [[The Second Dawn]]. Outbreaks once rare and localized now appear in nearly every settlement that relies on magic or lumitech, and infestations that once took generations to mature can now emerge within a matter of years. Whether the Second Dawn gave rise to a new form of corruption or simply inflamed one that had always existed remains disputed.

# Description
The corruption manifests as blue-violet, flesh-like growths threaded with veins of bioluminescent [[Lumir|lumir]]. Early infestations resemble translucent bruising or clusters of glowing veins spreading across walls, pipes, and machinery. As the blight matures, it develops nodules, tendrils, sensory organs, and reactive structures capable of independent movement.

# Behavior

Small Mageblight growths are common in cities, factories, and lower industrial districts. Most remain minor infestations little more than a persistent nuisance.

Typical signs of contamination include:

- flickering lamps,
- clocks chiming at irregular hours,
- tools activating without input,
- low humming within walls,
- and glowing veins spreading through nearby machinery.

Mageblight is drawn toward active lumir sources. As infestations grow, they become increasingly reactive, spreading through connected systems and absorbing nearby material into their mass. Mature growths may lash out at perceived threats or violently discharge unstable magic when disturbed.

At its worst, Mageblight can reshape entire landscapes. Regions consumed by severe outbreaks become warped environments of roaming aberrations born from the corruption itself.

No two blighted regions develop in exactly the same way. Some collapse into dense masses of pulsating organic growth, while others transform more radically depending on the nature of the corruption. The [[Glass Wastes]] became a barren expanse of crystalline formations and razor-edged glass dunes, while the [[Bleached Lands]] were stripped almost entirely of color, leaving behind a pale and lifeless landscape.

# Public Attitudes
In heavily industrialized regions, Mageblight is regarded as an unavoidable consequence of modern life. Most people are not especially afraid of it; for many citizens, minor infestations are viewed much like mold, leaking pipes, or industrial smog: unhealthy and unpleasant, but common enough to ignore until they become disruptive. In poorer districts especially, small growths often remain untreated for long periods.

Many households encounter low-grade contamination at some point in their lives. Common warning signs include:

- warm walls,
- soft humming,
- glowing cracks,
- or lumitech devices activating on their own.

Public perception is further shaped by how cities handle contaminated waste. Unstable lumitech and corrupted materials are frequently dumped into isolated disposal pits beyond city limits, where dormant or slow-growing Mageblight accumulates out of public sight. Most citizens rarely witness major infestations firsthand, leading many to underestimate how widespread the corruption truly is.

Among artificers, laborers, and maintenance crews, Mageblight has inspired countless superstitions. Some workshops leave lamps burning overnight to “settle” local lumir flows, while others play constant music in the belief that silence encourages blight growth.

# Cleansing
Cleansing Mageblight is rarely straightforward.

Destroying the visible growth alone is often ineffective. Though the flesh-like matter can be burned away or cut free, the corrupted lumir within an area may persist and later manifest elsewhere in a new form. An artificer is usually required to completely treat the Mageblight in lumitech.

Larger infestations are significantly more dangerous to remove. Dense growths can spread rapidly through nearby lumitech, release unstable magical discharges, or physically defend themselves when threatened.
