**Rathune, the Crimson Expanse**, is the [[Planes|plane]] of endless war and perpetual conflict, where fury never finds release. Rathune embodies anger without catharsis.

# Description

Rathune is a vast, scarred expanse of red-brown stone and ash-choked sky. The ground is fractured and uneven, split by trenches, impact craters, and rivers of smoldering slag. Smoke hangs perpetually in the air, diffusing a sunless crimson glow that never brightens nor dims. The terrain is sparse and brutal. Low hills of jagged rock break the horizon. Vast killing fields stretch between colossal fortifications, where siege engines and armies remain in constant motion. No civilians, untouched lands, or sanctuaries exist on the plane.

## The Elevated City
At the center of Rathune rises [[The Elevated City]], a colossal metropolis carved into and crowned atop a towering mesa visible from every battlefield on the horizon. Its walls are impossibly high, etched with the scars of ancient sieges. Its gates have shattered a thousand times and been remade a thousand more. At its heart lies an inner sanctum none have ever held for long. The city is ruled by [[The Arbiters|the Arbiters]], enigmatic custodians of perpetual war. They do not fight, yet no battle unfolds beyond their design. They do not choose victors, yet no victory endures without their consent. Through subtle decree and catastrophic intervention alike, they maintain the Balance of Fury.

A faction's influence within the city is determined by its success on the battlefield. The more territory and victories it claims, the greater its privileges:

- Access to arcane siege forges capable of crafting devastating war machines
- Authority to recruit powerful beings from beyond Rathune
- Strategic insight granted by the Arbiters themselves

It is said that true control of the Elevated City would grant authority over Rathune's destructive force: not merely the power to wage war, but the power to direct where that destruction falls across the planes. The factions fight for it not to end the war, but to become its master. A celestial claim to the city is watched more closely than any other, for it alone is said to threaten the war's end rather than merely its mastery.

When one faction grows too dominant, it inevitably turns its strength toward the inner stronghold of the Elevated City, seeking to claim the plane's ultimate destructive authority. The lesser factions form a desperate alliance, and the Arbiters themselves move to oppose the ascendant power. The Elevated City becomes the center of a catastrophic war unlike the endless battles below. Siege engines capable of reshaping landscapes are unleashed. Ancient forges awaken. The sky burns brighter than ever before. Yet, in recorded history, no faction has ever succeeded. The assault always collapses, through betrayal, exhaustion, Arbiter intervention, or the combined fury of the opposing forces. The dominant faction is broken, its power redistributed among its rivals, and the eternal equilibrium is restored.

## The War Below
Below the mesa, four great factions wage total war in brutal equilibrium. Every aspect of their existence is shaped by battle. Alliances form only when one faction threatens to gain advantage, and such truces inevitably collapse into betrayal. Among the four, only the celestial host claims that its warfare serves justice rather than conquest. Across the plane stand innumerable fortresses. Each is under constant siege. Each eventually falls. Ownership shifts endlessly. Banners are raised, torn down, and raised again in cycles that have repeated for eternity.

Death is not an escape. Slain warriors are eventually restored to the battlefield (sometimes diminished, sometimes twisted, but always ready to fight again). The strength of resurrection is not equal. When a warrior kills an enemy steeped in hatred for them, they steal a fragment of that enemy's essence. The deeper the enmity, the greater the theft, making grudges themselves a form of tactical advantage.

Individuals within these factions rise through the same cycle of victory and slaughter, climbing whatever hierarchy their army observes as they win. The strongest among the devils and demons become the [[Archdevils]] and [[Demon Lords]] who sit above their kind; the yugoloth produce their [[Ultroloths]], the celestial host its [[Solars]]. None of them stand apart from the war to rule over it: their standing is the rank they hold within their faction, and it lasts only as long as they can defend it. What they rise on is plunder as much as bloodshed. An individual who breaks a citadel inherits what its garrison could not defend (arms, wards, siege-forges), and every such theft feeds the same climb.

# Planar Interactions
Rathune is sustained by wrath, grudges, vengeance, and cruelty.

When Rathune accumulates excess emotional energy, it becomes increasingly resonant. The cycle of death and rebirth accelerates, sieges intensify, the sky burns brighter, and weapons strike harder. If resonance grows unchecked, even the Arbiters strain to maintain balance.

## Influence on the Material Plane
### Resonant
When Rathune becomes resonant with [[Arcanthys]], the [[Material Plane]] feels the pull of endless war. Conflicts ignite more easily and escalate more violently. Minor disputes spiral into bloodshed. Armies refuse surrender. Duels become executions. Magic tied to destruction, battle, and fury grows stronger. Healing magic falters. Spells that calm emotions or end conflict weaken or fail entirely.

In war-torn regions, fragments of Rathune may manifest physically. Duelling [[Demons]], [[Devils]], and [[Celestials]] may crash into mortal battlefields. Survivors report visions of the Elevated City in smoke and fire.

### Quiescent
When Rathune is quiescent, tempers cool more easily. Wars exhaust themselves sooner. Armies accept surrender.
