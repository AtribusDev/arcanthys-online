**The Everdeep** is the vast convergence of the dead at the heart of [[Noctyra, The Shadowfell]], where most souls that pass from life are gradually drawn and stripped of their identity. It forms the central stage of the [[Cycle of the Soul]] and the main reason resurrection grows harder the longer a soul remains within it.

# Description
The Everdeep takes the form of a vast, slow-moving swamp-river of soul sludge, stretching through the center of Noctyra. A muted green glow lights the sludge in a faint, sourceless twilight, enough to see by, but offering neither warmth nor any reliable sense of direction. Its drag wears down body and mind alike, pulling steadily at memory and sense of self the longer a creature remains caught in it. The pull is strong and dangerous, and only the most powerful magics offer a reliable means of escape once caught.

# The Fading of Identity
Souls drawn into the Everdeep do not retain their individuality indefinitely. The boundaries between them erode over time, and memory and identity grow steadily less distinct as one soul merges into the greater whole. This erosion is what sets the practical limits of [[Resurrection|resurrection]], covered in more detail on the [[Cycle of the Soul]], including what recovering a soul costs the caster attempting it.

This same pull makes the Everdeep dangerous to study directly. Its draw only grows stronger the closer one approaches, and those who venture too far in search of answers run an increasing risk of never returning.

# Undeath
Some souls slip free of the Everdeep's pull rather than dissolving into it, returning instead as shades or other undead that wander in search of the living. Undead created through especially powerful or deliberate means may go further still, reclaiming their own soul from within the Everdeep outright, though rarely without cost: prolonged exposure to the negative energy that sustains such a soul tends to warp it, driving the creature toward a colder, often outright evil nature. Even undead that merely cling to fragments of soul torn loose from the Everdeep, rather than reclaiming their own, are frequently left twisted in mind and memory.

# Origin
Tradition holds that the Everdeep, or at least the shape it now takes, dates to the later stages of the [[War of Shaping]], when the conflict between gods spread into Noctyra itself. According to the myth, three of [[The Fell Lords]] attempted to manipulate the Everdeep for their own ends, and a fourth deity, said to be [[Vhalis]], intervened. Outnumbered, Vhalis drew the three into the Everdeep's depths, and all four gods were destroyed there. Celestials and fiends are said to have first appeared in the planes shortly after, formed from what the Everdeep left of the fallen gods. The [[Lost Deities]] are remembered as the only gods known to have perished without return, and their end is closely tied to the Everdeep's reputation for consuming even the divine.

# Planar Influence
The Everdeep draws much of its strength from grief and apathy, the emotions that run strongest within it compared to the rest of Noctyra. When Noctyra grows resonant with [[Arcanthys]] in places heavy with these emotions, the Everdeep's touch shows through: spirits cross over more easily, and resurrection magic grows stronger, though far less predictable, than usual.
