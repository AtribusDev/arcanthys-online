The **Hall of the Worthy**, also called the Blooded Horizon, is the afterlife Gorvath Iron-Blood maintains for orcs who die well. Where mortal life under Gorvath's doctrine is a continuous test of an orc's "Inner Ore," the Hall is where that testing ends; combat there continues, but as celebration rather than trial, and the dead are no longer proving themselves so much as enjoying what they have already proven.

## The Blooded Horizon

The Hall lies within a plain called the Blooded Horizon, a stretch of golden-red grassland lit by a dusk that never fully falls. At its center stands the Great Hall of Iron Voices, a mead hall built of black iron, bone, and banners taken from a thousand battles. Its doors do not close, and the hall itself grows larger with every worthy soul who enters it. Laughter, boasting, and war-song carry through it without pause. Inside, long tables hold food and drink that never run dry, and old enemies sit side by side, bound now by mutual respect rather than old grudges.

## Daily Life in the Hall

### The Clashfields

Each day begins at the Clashfields, open ground where warriors gather to fight duels, skirmishes, and full warband battles. Wounds are real, but death here is neither final nor painful; a slain warrior simply returns to fight again. Losing carries no shame under Gorvath's doctrine, since only refusing to fight at all is treated as failure. What is won each morning is renown, not survival.

### The Feast of Deeds

By evening, warriors return to the Great Hall for the Feast of Deeds. Roasted meat, blood-broth, and strong drink appear without limit, and the day's battles are turned into song, usually enlarged well past what actually happened on the field. Victors boast and the defeated argue their case, but the exchange is treated as sport rather than insult. The greatest honor available in the Hall is having one's deeds retold by someone else, since among orcs a boast carries no weight until another voice repeats it.

## Gorvath's Presence

Unlike the distant god who tests the living, Gorvath is present within the Hall, though selectively so. He walks it in mortal guise and unannounced, and may sit beside an unremarkable warrior whose spirit has impressed him, or challenge a champion directly at a decisive moment. No one is told when they have met him; they learn it only afterward, if they were worthy of the meeting at all.

## Entry and Judgment

Passage into the Hall is earned, but by a standard more forgiving than the trials orcs face in life. It is generally understood to require having faced hardship directly, having proven strength through action rather than claim, and having refused a life of passivity or cowardice. Being the strongest is not the requirement; having genuinely tried to become strong is. The Ironbound, the priests who rule on whether a death is worthy or wasted, are held to carry that same judgment into who is recognized as having earned a place in the Hall.

## Standing Among the Dead

The Hall keeps no fixed ranks, but reputation still carries weight after death. Warriors of great renown gather followings, old rivalries continue across eternity, and a handful of figures have become near-mythic within the Hall's memory. No standing there is permanent, however; even the most storied warrior in the Hall can still be challenged, and can still lose.

## Contested Accounts

Not every account of the Hall agrees. [[Kharzug One-Eye]], who claims to have died and returned from Gorvath's afterlife, describes a hall grown overcrowded with the unworthy, softened by endless feasting into hollow victories rather than earned ones. His account underlies the Final Proving, the civil war he preached to the [[Unbroken Host]] as a means of purging both the living Host and the Hall itself. Whether Kharzug's description reflects a genuine flaw in the Hall or a self-serving justification for the war that broke the Unbroken Host remains disputed among orcs to this day.

## The Edge of the Horizon

Past the Great Hall, the golden-red plain runs on to a horizon that never resolves into anything beyond it. Some say the greatest warriors in the Hall eventually leave its feasts and duels to walk into that horizon in search of one final battle, one that not even Gorvath speaks of. No one who has gone that way has returned, and what, if anything, waits there remains unknown.
