**Titles:** The Proving Flame, Father of the Worthy, Breaker of the Unproven
**Alignment:** Lawful Neutral, with strong leanings toward honor-bound brutality
**Domains:** War, Forge, Strength, Order
**Symbol:** A clenched fist gripping a broken blade, reforged mid-strike

---

### Origin Myth

Orc tradition holds that Gorvath arose not as a creator but as a challenger, believing that existence held no meaning unless it was earned. He struck the earth with his hammer, and from the molten scars emerged the first orcs, beings not born but forged, each incomplete and flawed by design. Gorvath's decree is recorded as: "Only those who prove their strength deserve to endure. All else must be broken, or remade." From this, orcs regard themselves not as a chosen people but as a people under ongoing test.

### Core Beliefs of the Faith

Orc society shaped by Gorvath is organized around a martial meritocracy. Rank is earned through combat, leadership, or achievement rather than inherited through birth. Defeat is expected and not considered shameful; only stagnation, the refusal to improve, is condemned. War, duels, and trials carry religious as well as political weight, and leadership is treated as conditional: any leader may be challenged, and a loss is taken as proof of unworthiness.

### The Forging Doctrine

Central to worship is the belief that every orc carries an "Inner Ore," a potential that must be refined through hardship. Priests teach the process in four stages: battle serves as the hammer, pain as the fire, failure as the folding of the metal, and victory as the sharpening of the blade. A life without struggle is considered spiritually unforged.

### Clergy: The Ironbound

Gorvath's priests are warrior-smiths known as the Ironbound. They judge duels and trials, reforge the weapons of fallen warriors into relics, and rule on whether a death was worthy or wasted. Some Ironbound deliberately provoke conflict to prevent what they see as societal weakness, and high-ranking members have been known to sabotage or impose harsh trials on a tribe in order to purify it.

### Rituals and Practices

Young orcs undergo a ritual battle called the First Scar, intended to leave their first real wound as proof that their forging has begun. A defeated warrior may undergo the Ash Trial, returning from exile stronger or not returning at all. The Reforging is the practice of melting down the weapons of the fallen and redistributing the metal, symbolizing that strength never dies, only changes hands.

### View of Other Races

Elves are regarded as stagnant and overly refined, described by Gorvath's faithful as metal polished but never tested. Dwarves are respected, though pitied for valuing craft over conflict. Humans are considered intriguing and potentially strong, but are often seen as wasting their forging.

### Internal Conflict

Orc interpretation of Gorvath's doctrine is not uniform. The Pure Iron hold that only constant war proves worth, and act as aggressive expansionists. The Tempered Path teach that restraint and discipline are the higher forms of strength. The Ashborn are a radical sect holding that most orcs are failures who must be culled. These divisions have produced lasting political tension, civil wars, and ideological conflict within orc society.

### Divine Behavior

Gorvath is not held to answer prayers for mercy or safety. He is said to empower those who fight against overwhelming odds, to break the weapons of those he deems unworthy, and to send visions before major battles that offer no clear answers. Among his worshippers, his blessing is commonly understood to arrive in the form of a harder challenge.
