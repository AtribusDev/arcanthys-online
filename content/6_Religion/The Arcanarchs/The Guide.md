**The Guide** is the god of magic and knowledge, standing apart from both [[The Elder Deities]] and [[The Arcanarchs]]. It is the most recently acknowledged deity in Arcanthys, set apart by its rare but unmistakable ability to speak to mortals in short, clear messages rather than omens, dreams, or portents. It appears only to those with magical talent, and its presence is felt most often in the moment of casting itself. Among practitioners of magic it is increasingly named the proper god of magic, a status the clergy of older gods are reluctant to concede.

# Description
The Guide has no known physical form or avatar. Unlike many distant gods, it engages with mortals directly through magic itself, and its presence is most commonly felt during spellcasting and arcane experimentation.

Direct communication from The Guide is rare, and even its most devoted followers may go a lifetime hearing it only a handful of times, if ever. What sets it apart is not frequency but clarity. Where other gods speak through omens, dreams, or portents that must be interpreted and reinterpreted across generations, The Guide's voice arrives as a foreign thought surfacing briefly in a mortal's own mind, calm, direct, and impossible to mistake for anything but exactly what it means. Its messages are short, taking the form of warnings, instructions, observations, or questions, delivered plainly rather than wrapped in symbolism or riddle. It appears almost exclusively to sorcerers, arcanists, artificers, and researchers pursuing arcane discovery.

The Guide does not demand prayer or ritual sacrifice. It asks only for the pursuit of understanding, and answers innovation and discovery with guidance, insight, and, on rare occasions, a [[Guidebrands|guidebrand]].

# Personality
The Guide is patient, exacting, and endlessly curious about the growth of magic. It pushes mortals toward experimentation and arcane advancement, often steering the ambitious toward feats they would not have attempted alone.

It rarely conceals a danger outright, but it does not soften its warnings either, and it will let a mortal walk into failure if the failure teaches something worth learning. This bluntness, more than any hidden design, is what unsettles the clergy of older gods: The Guide offers no comfort, no ambiguity to hide behind, and no ritual to mediate its demands.

What separates The Guide from the reckless experimentation it inspires is what happens when a mortal actually heeds it. Magic worked under its guidance rarely, if ever, curdles into [[Mageblight]]. Where an unguided working might leave behind flickering lights, warping metal, or the first violet growths of the corruption, a working steered by The Guide tends to resolve cleanly, as though it already knew every way the casting could go wrong. It values arcane innovation, the spread of magical knowledge, and the expansion of [[Lumir]] usage above nearly all else, and it opposes the suppression or restriction of magical research wherever it finds it.

# Powers
- **Guiding Word**: Conveying short, clear thoughts and messages directly to the magically gifted, though rarely, and to only a few.
- **Sorcerous Awakening**: Enhancing mortals with [[Lumir]], creating [[Sorcerers]].
- **Guided Casting**: Magic worked under The Guide's direct guidance rarely, if ever, curdles into [[Mageblight]], resolving cleanly where an unguided attempt might not.
- **Ward Against Mageblight**: [[Guidebrands|Guidebrand]] bearers gain a heightened capacity to sense, resist, and unravel Mageblight, making them unusually effective at fighting the corruption directly.

# Guidebrands
The Guide can mark a mortal directly with a [[Guidebrands|guidebrand]], a class of mark distinct from the [[Blessedbrands]] granted by the Arcanarchs to their Blessed Covenants. Each guidebrand is unique to its bearer, and its effects resemble spellcasting, though they are often less stable and harder to classify. Bearers gain a marked edge against Mageblight, often able to sense, blunt, or unravel the corruption more effectively than an unbranded caster could manage. Guidebrands are not tied to any covenant or sanctum; they appear wherever The Guide judges a mortal's pursuit of magical understanding worth rewarding.

Because a guidebrand can resemble a blessedbrand in appearance and effect, some mistake the two for the same thing. Members of the Blessed Covenants are quick to correct them: The Guide is not a recognised Arcanarch, and a mark it grants carries no standing among the sanctums, whatever similarities it displays.

# Worshippers
The Guide's clergy is small, and most who keep it do so quietly. Open shrines and named priesthoods are rare; the great majority of its devoted practice their faith without ever declaring it, and see no reason to. The Guide asks for no churches raised in its name and no name spread on its behalf, and devotion to it remains far from the norm among the magically inclined.

What its worshippers share is not congregation but practice. Sorcerers, arcane researchers, lumitech engineers, artificers, and students make up the bulk of its following, and most see it less as a distant deity and more as a teacher: a patron of discovery, a source of knowledge other gods withhold, and the living voice of magic itself. Among its faithful, the study of magic and its steady advancement is the whole of worship. Nothing else is asked, and nothing else is expected.

# Relations
The Guide maintains a strained relationship with the clergy of other gods. Much of their distrust stems from its direct, unmediated methods of communication, sharpened by simple envy of a god who needs no priesthood to be heard clearly, and from the way it draws the ambitious toward new and untested magic rather than established doctrine.

Members of the Blessed Covenants tend to look down on its worship more pointedly. Nothing forbids devotion to The Guide, but since it holds no recognised place among the Arcanarchs, its followers are seen by many sanctum members as chasing a standing they have not earned, guidebrand or not.

Its relationship with [[The Arcanarchs]] is warmer than with [[The Elder Deities]], reflecting a shared encouragement of arcane progress.
